# Citations

Sources used for medical statements on the Keratoconus Vision Simulator. Numbered in **Vancouver (NLM)** style, the format used by PubMed and most clinical journals. The same numbers appear as superscripts on the site.

This simulator is an illustration of possible visual effects. It is not a diagnostic tool and does not replace examination by an eye-care professional.

## How numbers are used on the page

Prevalence is reported as a **range**, not a single world figure. Older studies often found roughly 50–230 cases per 100,000 people.² A 2020 meta-analysis pooled 1.38 per 1,000.⁴ A Dutch registry study found about 1 in 375.³ A 2025 global meta-analysis pooled about 0.24%.⁹ Some Middle Eastern series report much higher rates.¹,⁵ Differences come from ethnicity, age mix, and whether diagnosis used older keratometry or modern tomography.

## What this simulator does *not* claim

- It does not diagnose keratoconus or grade severity.
- Ghost, scatter, and blur sliders are phenomenological: they are inspired by coma-dominated higher-order aberration and irregular astigmatism,⁶,⁷ not a patient-specific wavefront.
- Individual eyes differ. Bilateral disease is often asymmetric.²
